Meet Vera Rubin. This is episode eight of From Sand to GPU.

Vera Rubin is NVIDIA’s rack-scale AI platform succeeding Grace Blackwell-era systems. It co-designs multiple chips so the rack — and the POD — is the unit of compute. That framing is confirmed from NVIDIA Newsroom and the NVIDIA Technical Blog in early twenty-twenty-six.

Naming first. Vera is the custom Arm-compatible CPU with Olympus cores, on a successor trajectory to Grace. Rubin is the GPU accelerator with HBM4 and Transformer Engine. Vera Rubin platform means the full chip, rack, networking, storage, and software stack. NVL72 is the flagship rack integrating seventy-two Rubin GPUs and thirty-six Vera CPUs.

NVIDIA states the platform brings together Vera CPU, Rubin GPU, NVLink 6 Switch, ConnectX-9 SuperNIC, BlueField-4 DPU, and Spectrum-6 Ethernet switch. A GTC twenty-twenty-six update newly integrates NVIDIA Groq 3 LPU as a seventh chip. The Technical Blog initially framed six new chips; the Newsroom update adds Groq 3 — confirmed.

Public rack SKUs include Vera Rubin NVL72; a Vera CPU Rack with two-hundred-fifty-six Vera CPUs in a liquid-cooled MGX form; a Groq 3 LPX Rack with two-hundred-fifty-six LPU processors; a BlueField-4 STX Storage Rack for AI-native KV-cache and context memory; and a Spectrum-6 SPX Ethernet Rack for scale-out, configurable for Spectrum-X or Quantum-X800 InfiniBand. Partner products start in the second half of twenty-twenty-six — confirmed. Named clouds include AWS, Google Cloud, Azure, and OCI, plus several GPU cloud providers.

Selected confirmed figures from NVIDIA tables: Rubin carries three-hundred-thirty-six billion transistors on the full chip, versus two-hundred-eight billion cited for Blackwell; two compute dies; fifty PFLOPS NVFP4 inference and thirty-five PFLOPS NVFP4 training; up to two-hundred-eighty-eight gigabytes of HBM4 at twenty-two terabytes per second per GPU; and three-point-six terabytes per second bidirectional NVLink 6 per GPU. Vera offers eighty-eight Olympus cores and one-hundred-seventy-six spatial multithreads, up to one-point-five terabytes of LPDDR5X at up to one-point-two terabytes per second, and one-point-eight terabytes per second NVLink-C2C. NVL72 is seventy-two GPUs plus thirty-six CPUs.

Honesty box for trackers: platform chip list, NVL72 composition, HBM4, and NVLink 6 bandwidths are confirmed from NVIDIA primary sources. TSMC process node for Rubin — for example N3 or N3P — is likely in industry press but not consistently printed on the NVIDIA newsroom pages reviewed; treat node as non-confirmed until NVIDIA or TSMC primary. CoWoS-L as packaging vehicle is likely from press and prior Blackwell path, but unknown as a NVIDIA product-page one-liner. Exact HBM stack count, often discussed as eight, is likely secondary. Die size in square millimeters and unit BOM dollar cost are unknown publicly in the sources reviewed.
