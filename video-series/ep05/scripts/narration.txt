HBM, substrates, and bridges. This is episode five of From Sand to GPU.

Advanced packaging only works when three distinct supply chains arrive on time: high-bandwidth memory stacks, an interposer or local silicon bridges, and a high-layer-count organic package substrate.

Start with HBM. DRAM makers build three-D-stacked memory with a base die and through-silicon vias, sold as stacks that sit beside the GPU on the interposer or RDL. HBM3E was the volume workhorse into the mid-twenty-twenties. HBM4 is next. NVIDIA’s Rubin materials publicly pair the GPU with HBM4 — that pairing is confirmed. The three major vendors are SK hynix, Samsung, and Micron — confirmed as the industry set. Which vendor is qualified for which customer at which moment is time-varying.

Next, the interconnect plane. CoWoS-S uses a large silicon interposer to redistribute signals between logic and HBM. CoWoS-L uses smaller local silicon interconnect bridges plus organic RDL to span multi-reticle assemblies — that description is likely per packaging analyses. Interposer area scales poorly, which is why CoWoS-L adoption narratives keep showing up.

Beneath that assembly sits the organic package substrate — typically an advanced flip-chip BGA that uses Ajinomoto Build-up Film, or ABF, as the critical dielectric. Ajinomoto’s IR discusses ABF as interlayer insulating material for logic IC package substrates — confirmed product role. Substrate fabricators commonly named include Unimicron, Ibiden, Shinko, Nan Ya PCB, Samsung Electro-Mechanics, and AT and S — a likely leading set, not an exhaustive certified list.

Materials dependencies stack quickly: HBM needs DRAM wafers, TSV, and bonding at the memory IDM. Interposers consume extra three-hundred-millimeter silicon. Bridges are small silicon dies with fine interconnect. ABF film comes from varnish to film. Copper, glass cloth, and laminate feed substrate makers. Solder bumps and balls close the mechanical stack.

Geography matters. HBM capacity is centered in Korea with mixed Micron footprints. ABF film is Japan-centric. Substrates cluster in Taiwan, Japan, Korea, and Europe. Interposer flow often sits inside TSMC’s packaging path in Taiwan.

Public NVIDIA figures for Rubin-class packages cite up to two-hundred-eighty-eight gigabytes of HBM4 and twenty-two terabytes per second per GPU — confirmed from NVIDIA Developer Blog and NVL72 product tables. Stack count is commonly discussed as eight stacks in secondary writeups — likely; confirm against official datasheet line items before locking a BOM quantity. Exact public split of Rubin HBM4 supply among the three vendors is unknown — contractual and rarely disclosed.

Risks: HBM qualification is customer- and generation-specific, so dual-sourcing is slow. ABF and large substrates have been historical bottlenecks; twenty-twenty-six coverage describes renewed CapEx at major substrate makers — CapEx dollar figures in press are estimated unless primary. Substrate quality gates signal integrity for HBM and SerDes escaping the package.

Trackers should watch HBM supplier earnings and sold-out commentary, NVIDIA’s three-vendor qualification health, Ajinomoto ABF CapEx, substrate lead times and layer counts for AI packages, and the HBM4 twelve-high versus eight-high mix.
